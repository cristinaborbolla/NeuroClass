Alzheimer's disease staging

Alzheimer's disease progresses through identifiable stages with characteristic changes in brain structure and cognitive function. Understanding these stages is key to early diagnosis and intervention.

The four diagnostic stages used in this classifier

1
Non-Demented No impairment ▼

No significant cognitive impairment is present. The individual maintains normal memory function, orientation, and daily living activities. Brain structure appears within expected ranges for the given age group, with cortical thickness and ventricular volume showing no pathological signs.

Regular monitoring is still recommended for individuals over 65 or with a family history of Alzheimer's disease.

Normal memory function
Intact daily activities
No cortical atrophy
Normal ventricular size
2
Very Mild Demented Very mild decline ▼

Subtle cognitive changes begin to appear, primarily affecting memory for recent events and the ability to recall names. These changes may be attributed to normal ageing by both the patient and their family, making early detection particularly challenging.

Structural MRI may show minimal hippocampal volume reduction. The patient typically remains independent in daily activities but may exhibit mild forgetfulness.

Occasional forgetfulness
Mostly independent
Subtle hippocampal changes
Early monitoring advised
3
Mild Demented Mild decline ▼

Memory loss becomes more noticeable and begins to interfere with daily activities. The patient may have difficulty with complex tasks, show increased confusion about time and place, and struggle with problem-solving. Social withdrawal may begin at this stage.

Brain imaging typically reveals enlarged ventricles and measurable reduction in hippocampal and cortical volume, particularly in the temporal and parietal lobes.

Noticeable memory loss
Difficulty with complex tasks
Enlarged ventricles
Temporal lobe atrophy
4
Moderate Demented Moderate decline ▼

Significant cognitive decline affecting multiple domains. The patient requires assistance with daily activities and may not recognise close family members. Behavioural changes such as agitation, wandering, and sleep disturbances are common. Full-time care is often necessary at this stage.

Structural MRI shows significant diffuse cortical atrophy, substantially enlarged ventricles, and marked hippocampal volume loss. These changes are typically visible and measurable on standard imaging.

Requires daily assistance
Recognition difficulties
Significant cortical atrophy
Full care often needed

🔬 How the model works

  • Input: axial brain MRI slice (grayscale)
  • Preprocessing: CLAHE contrast enhancement
  • Resolution: 190 × 200 pixels
  • Architecture: custom CNN (Advanced-Reduced)
  • Output: softmax probabilities over 4 classes
  • Training dataset: Kaggle Augmented Alzheimer MRI
